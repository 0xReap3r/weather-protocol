Stormline is not an official warning service. In danger? Call 911. Follow the National Weather Service, emergency managers and local officials. Keep Wireless Emergency Alerts on.

1. What Stormline is

A free map that brings together public weather and hazard data and reports from neighbors. By using it you agree to these terms.

2. Information can be wrong or late

  • Official data can be delayed, incomplete or unavailable, and the map can lag behind official warnings.
  • Neighbor reports are unverified. One report shows faintly until another neighbor confirms it; even confirmed reports can be wrong.
  • Satellite, radar and model data are estimates. Gray or empty areas mean "no data," never "safe."
  • Push alerts can arrive late or not at all. Don't rely on them alone.
  • Route checks only look at warnings and neighbor reports. They can't see every flooded street, closure or hazard. Never drive into water: turn around, don't drown.

3. Reporting

  • Report only what you see, and only when it's safe. Never wade or drive into water to check it.
  • Don't post false reports or use reports to harass anyone. Reports hold no free text or photos, so don't try to share personal information through them.
  • We may hide or remove reports, limit how often you can report, or block abuse.
  • The SOS link calls 911. Stormline doesn't send rescue requests or contact emergency services for you.
